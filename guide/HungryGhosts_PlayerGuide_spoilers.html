How the Game Works生き抜く為の心得

Hungry Ghosts tells you very little. This guide explains how its systems work: the controls, the two gauges, what the menus show, how items, doors and saving behave, and what the game keeps count of. It is meant for when you are lost or unsure how something works. It is not a walkthrough, and it keeps the story out of it.

It names things as Mord of Swoonlight's English fan translation does, with the Japanese in the table at the end. Its companions:

  • Dangers: the ghosts, curses, possession, the Reapers and the graveyards;
  • Places: an atlas of the areas and how they connect;
  • Items: every item, what it does and what it costs;
  • Fifteen Judgments: the endings, and how to steer toward each one.

Where this comes from. Mord of Swoonlight's fan-translation project read these rules out of the game's own code and data, and checked many of them against save files in play. Much of the advice is the game's own, from its Information pages (section 8); those are quoted from the translation.

1The shape of the game

You wander a land of the dead, and nothing about it is explained. There are no levels and no experience points. What the game keeps count of is:

  • your Fate, which rises with what you do and choose;
  • three desires: Greed, Attachment and Destruction;
  • the bonds you form with the dead you meet.

At the end you are judged on those, and the judgment picks one of fifteen endings.

The world changes with your bonds. In the game's own words: "This is no ordinary 'surface' world. The Village ever shifts. Paths change with new bonds. The more you engage, the more await." Doors that were sealed can open, ghosts appear or don't, and a few choices close paths for the rest of the playthrough. The game itself is short if you hurry; most of it lies off the quick road.

At New Game you name your character. The name turns up in the game later.

Fighting with the right stick:

  • Push the stick up to thrust with a spear. Push again quickly for a combo.
    • The Steel and Alloy Shafts allow rapid combos: keep the stick held up.
  • Pull the stick all the way back to draw the spear back and charge it. In the game's words: "Draw the spear back; ki grows. Ki-charged blows hit harder."
  • Push the stick to throw what you're holding.
  • Crossbows: hold the stick back to nock a bolt, then push it forward to shoot, much like a charged spear attack. Shoot before the bolt is properly nocked and it misfires.

When something grabs you, waggle both sticks hard to break free. "Panic breeds mistakes. Act with a cool head."

3Spirit and Vitality

Two gauges sit at the top of the screen. The game describes them on its very first Information page:

gaugewheredrained byin the game's words
Spiritupper left, bluemental harm"Low Spirit leaves you defenseless against all harm"
Vitalityright, redblows"Low Vitality means death itself"

When Spirit runs out, the game tells you: "All Spirit lost… Cannot charge attacks, now weak to curses…" Blows to your Vitality also do a fifth more harm until you restore some Spirit. That's what "defenseless" amounts to: you don't die of it, but everything gets more dangerous. Spirit is also what keeps curses off you, the higher the better (the Dangers guide has the numbers).

When Vitality runs out, you die.

  • With a Reaper's Tooth in your items, it crumbles and you get back up instead, usually where you fell, sometimes back at the doorway. The Tooth also lifts every curse, ends a possession, and brings Spirit and Vitality back to at least half. Each Tooth saves you once. It can't save you from a death sentence whose time has run out (see the Dangers guide).
  • Without one, the game is over. A picture of your end, then the title screen, where Continue loads a saved game.

Restoring them:

  • Foods and salves restore Vitality or Spirit: most a little, a Beehive or a Fresh Salve fully.
  • Rotten fruits, the Black Fruit, the Rotten Beehive and the foul salves lower your gauges instead, but end a possession (their descriptions say they "lift an affliction"; see the Dangers guide). Spotted mushrooms and apples are just poison.
  • Soul Shards restore both gauges fully. They are orbs of light, and a short scene plays when you take one; they come from the bonds you forge with the dead. The journal calls them the "power to survive" sealed in the malice of the dead, and says stronger ones remain "where malice binds deepest". The menu screen counts them, up to 99.
  • Your maximum Spirit grows by 10 at a time, from 50 up to 100: the game says "Max Spirit increased…" and the blue gauge gains flames.

Protection: a LifeWard halves the harm to your Vitality and a SoulWard halves the harm to your Spirit, just by being carried. (Their descriptions: "Just holding it, Vitality takes less harm," and the same for Spirit.)

4Looking, touching, taking

Walk up to something and press ○. The commands for it appear: Look, Open, Take, Use and so on.

  • Hand commands such as Open or Take put your arm out. Push the right stick to reach, pull it back to withdraw.
  • Inspect everything. The game insists on it: "'Inspect' is a vital way to survive this world, for indifference is most deadly," and "All begins with 'knowing'." Writing on walls, bodies, objects and corners often holds what you need.
  • Dark places hide things. "Strain to search as you might, you discern little. The fix is obvious: make light, then search." Light a match, or carry the Dark Eye, which lets you see in the dark.
  • Hidden events. Carrying the Third Eye lets you see the hidden side of objects and tools. The Hell's Eye does the same in the graveyards: with it, a Hell's Eye choice joins Inspect on the command wheel. "Your 「Fate」 may be swayed…"

The text colors mean something.

  • Red words are key words: clues, item names, the names the dead give you.
  • Yellow text marks a conversation that the game files in your journal's Memories (section 8).

5Weapons, tools and liquids

You fight with what you have equipped. The item menu (section 6) sets one weapon and one tool, and □ switches between them and empty hands.

Weapons:

  • Spears are a shaft plus a tip.
    • Shafts: Wood, Steel, Alloy. The shaft doesn't change how hard you hit; the tip and charging do. The Steel and Alloy Shafts let you follow a thrust with more (combos), and the Alloy Shaft charges in half the time (1 second instead of 2).
    • Tips: Iron Tip; Blood Tip, which holds strong spirit power; Torch Tip, wrapped in oilcloth, which catches fire in flame.
    • A charged blow does three to five times the damage of a plain one, but only while you have some Spirit. Aimed at the head, it can drop a graveyard ghoul in one hit.
  • Crossbows (Hunter, War and Knight) fire Arrows, Iron Arrows and Fire Arrows. The better ones load and fire faster.
  • The Grave Gun, won in the graveyards, never runs out of ammunition.

The game warns that "A weapon's efficacy is unclear": ghosts differ, and "effectiveness depends on type. Of fire, of blood, of dark…" (the Dangers guide covers each kind).

Liquids spread and react. "Oil burns; water kills red flame. Blood lures; step on it, dead rise." A pool changes when another liquid meets it. Blood is dangerous to walk through, and fire and water can open or close a way.

Throwing food distracts the starving dead, and "they loathe a foul stench": smell works on every ghost.

6The item menu (△)

The item menu has four tabs: Weapons, Equip Items (tools), Use Items and the Old Journal. Look at an item for its description, Equip it or Use it.

Some items work just by being carried:

Others you use: foods and salves (section 3); effigies, which lift curses; the Cracked Bell, whose ringing destroys a curse set in an object; and the Grave Handbell, which calls someone in the graveyard.

Everything you take has a price. "Here, many things can be had. Here, none will fault you for it. But it's a choice: how far to drown in desire, or how much you resist it." Picking things up raises your desires (section 10), and whatever you still carry at the end counts again. The game asks: "What do you carry? Is it something you need? Is it excessive?" You can drop things from the item menu.

Boxes:

  • A skull box opens with a Skull Key and offers several items, but you may take only one. The rest vanish with the box.
  • A jewelbox opens with a Jewelbox Key. Some let you take only one of two.

Keys are spent from the item menu at a lock. The Unknown Key opens a locked door, but "not every door seems to open". Each key used adds to your Fate.

7The menu screen (Select)

Select opens the menu screen. It shows your equipped weapon and tool, your Soul Shards and the time played, and has four choices:

  • Select Item: the item menu.
  • Show HUD: how much of the on-screen display to show: all of it, part of it, or nothing at all for a clean, cinematic screen.
  • Old Journal: section 8.
  • Mirror: look at yourself. It has three tabs:
    • Possession: whether something has possessed you. "Not possessed for now…" means clean.
    • Desire: your three desire tallies as numbers. The Desire Wheel adds a page of text for each.
    • Curses: what curses you, if anything. "Not cursed for now…" means clean.

Your Fate shows here only while you carry a Fate Wheel. Without one, you first see it at the judgment.

8The Old Journal

The journal fills itself as you go. It has three sections:

  • Information: the pages you pick up, and the notices you read on walls. They hold the game's advice, its tutorials and many of its hints. When you're stuck, reread them; they often say exactly where to look.
  • Memories: a summary of each important conversation, filed when the text turns yellow.
  • Tales: purple parchments. In the game's words: "The purple parchment speaks: at times, a way forward; at times, what your choice closed; and at times, it can even bend what lies ahead."

Learning something changes the world. "Find and learn a Tale or memento: a change stirs 'somewhere'. If you never find them, the hidden bonds stay unknown." Some ghosts say more, or ask different things, once you know their Tale.

Picking up a page adds Fate and a little Attachment. Reading a notice on a wall records it but adds no Fate. Some pages, many of them in the graveyards, are read on the spot and not kept: "Not filed in 『Old Journal』." That's normal.

9Meeting the dead

Most of the game's story is in its meetings with ghosts, and most of its choices are there too.

  • Talk to them, more than once. What they say depends on what you know and what you carry.
  • Replies come up as a short list. Some answers can't be taken back.
  • When a ghost asks for something, a list of your items comes up. Give one, or give nothing. What you hand over, and to whom, is among the biggest choices in the game.
  • Keepsakes (letters, photos, rings, paintings) stir the memories of the dead, and "Relics emit feelings… What to do with them is yours to choose. And so the road ahead shifts with that karma."

Scenes pay Fate, the best ones by far the most of anything in the game. Some give a Soul Shard too.

10Fate and the desires

Fate is "power to open the future… Only by acts and choices that defy fate can this power grow." It only ever goes up. It rises with:

  • the ghosts' scenes and their outcomes;
  • keepsakes, pages and keys: picking them up, and using keys;
  • every room and area you enter, and every door you open, the first time;
  • defeated ghosts.

Two doors test your Fate. Leaving the first area needs 40,000 Fate, and leaving the prisons needs 140,000. Short of that, the door sends you back to First Land: go and do more first.

The three desires are running tallies that the game reads at the end:

  • Greed: taking things, food above all;
  • Attachment: reading and keeping pages, carrying protective items, and saving;
  • Destruction: fighting, smashing things, taking up weapons.

Ghosts work on them as well: "Facing a ghost, you shift your three desires." The Mirror shows the tallies. Only a few things in the game lower them; Fifteen Judgments lists them.

The Seven Lures from Hell are seven deeds the judgment holds against you. The game lists them on one of its Information pages. Fifteen Judgments names all seven; one is easy to fall into early: picking up a Purple Effigy.

11Doors and the way forward

  • Locked doors need a key; a few need something else, such as a lever arm or an offering. The first time you open each door adds a little Fate.
  • Fate Doors go one way. "Once through it, you cannot turn back at will." "Past one door, the Fate Door is set… Before you pass it, finish what you must…" Before stepping through, make sure you've done what you wanted in the area behind you.
  • The doors read your deeds. "Passing many of these you reach Judgment Gate. But your prior deeds change what's beyond." Which door appears, and where it leads, depends on the bonds you've formed.
    • Leaving the first area without taking up any of its ghosts' stories skips most of the game. A keepsake from one of them is enough to stay on the long road. The game warns: "Seek hidden karma, or else few soul shards and little Fate Power come. But the path to Judgment Gate is still quick and easy."
  • A wall of fate blocks a way you haven't earned. "A wall of fate means no bond with what waits beyond. Did you miss bonding with someone? Did you not find a bound item?"
  • Some walls give way. A wall can hide a room, and the right liquid or fire can weaken it. The Information pages say which.

12Saving and loading

  • The game offers a save when you go through a door into another room. There's no save command: say yes at that prompt, or carry on. No offer comes:
    • in the first room after starting or loading;
    • on the boat;
    • at the judgment;
    • when moving within a graveyard.
  • Every save adds 200 Attachment. Save when you need to, not by habit.
  • To load, use Continue on the title screen, or the pause menu (Start) during play.
  • A loaded game starts at the doorway you came into the room by, not where you stood.
  • Each save file is about 475 KB.

13What the world remembers

  • Rooms remember. Ghosts defeated, items taken, things broken and doors opened stay that way when you leave and come back.
  • But some ghosts come back. Some rooms refill their ghosts a while after you clear them: most after about 15 seconds, some after several minutes. Every kill adds Fate, and Destruction too.
  • The graveyards forget. They start fresh on each visit.
  • Choices stick. What you gave, took, kept or refused shapes later scenes. A few choices are final for the whole playthrough; Fifteen Judgments lists them as points of no return.

14When you're stuck

  1. Reread the Old Journal. The Information pages and Tales are the game's hints, and they're often specific.
  2. Inspect again, especially dark corners, bodies, wall writing and anything odd. Make light first.
  3. Look at what you carry. A ghost may want one of your items. Talk to the ones you've met again.
  4. Check your Fate if a door keeps sending you back to First Land.
  5. Check the Mirror for a curse or a possession: either can stop you from using items, searching or moving freely.
  6. Try the liquids: blood, water, oil and fire change things.
  7. Remember the one-way doors. If an area is closed off behind you, what's left is ahead.

1The ghosts

Ghosts differ. "Effectiveness depends on type. Discern it to survive. Of fire, of blood, of dark, and…" The game's hints on their natures:

  • "Flame, blinding light, foul stench and red blood. Drawn by light or dark; hate odd smell; stirred by blood."
  • "Some hate fire, others hate water. Learn their natures, use wisely."
  • "Scented things and light sources are what the ghosts here loathe."
  • The starving dead chase thrown food, and "they loathe a foul stench. Smell especially works on every ghost."

What works on them:

  • Daggers hurt only the dead and ghosts.
  • A charged spear blow is "a rare way to repel evil beings": pull the spear back, then strike.
  • The Pale Fruit makes evil things recoil, a Sachet's scent drives them off, and a Threadball slows them for a while.

When one grabs you, waggle both sticks hard to break free.

Fighting has a cost. Each ghost you defeat adds Fate, and Destruction too. "Facing a ghost, you shift your three desires." You can often slip past instead.

Some come back.

  • Ghosts placed in a room stay dead once defeated.
  • Many rooms also draw ghosts from a hidden source that refills a while after you clear it: most after about 15 seconds, some after several minutes. A room you've cleared can fill up again while you stand in it.

Blood wakes the dead. "Tread on blood, and dead rise." Pools of blood bring ghosts up, and a Red Bag thrown into a puddle can do the same on purpose. "Mind where corpses fall… On blood, they stir. On fire, they recall agony."

Light and dark draw different things. "Dark summons, light summons. Flame illuminates the dark. Kill light to make dark."

  • The Iron Masks are "enforcers ruled by desire. They favor the darkness, and vanish in light." Lighting a place can clear them out.
  • Some other things only come out in the light.

Ambushes. "Something near… Just my imagination…?" or "Someone behind me…" is no idle thought: ghosts are about to appear around you, sometimes several at once. Turn and get ready.

Thieving hands. In some places hands reach out of a door and snatch an item from you ("「Apple」 was taken…"), and they keep grabbing. Strike them and they withdraw.

The skulls on pikes and ledges only mock you as you pass.

Hanged bodies. Some hanging corpses can be struck until they break. Leave them be: destroying one counts against you at the judgment (Fifteen Judgments, the Seven Lures).

2Curses

"Here, many things cast curses." A curse is the grudge of someone who died a certain death, and it hangs on you until it's lifted. The Mirror's Curses tab shows what curses you; when you're clean it says "Not cursed for now…".

Spirit is your defense

Whether a curse takes hold depends on your Spirit (the blue gauge):

  • at or below a low mark, the curse always takes;
  • at or above a high mark, it can't take;
  • in between, it's a chance, from 25% to 65%.

The marks depend on the curse and on how strong its source is:

cursesalways take at Spiritcan't take at Spirit
Darkness, Jewelry Box, Burn at Stake, Slaughter10 to 35 or less40 to 70 or more
Buried Alive, Glutton, 1000 Needles, Skeleton15 to 45 or less40 to 80 or more
Paralysis, Hunger, Blood20 to 60 or less40 to 90 or more

Your maximum Spirit starts at 50. It grows by 10 at a time, up to 100, each time the game says "Max Spirit increased…" (the blue gauge gets more flames). So keep Spirit up, and most weak curses can't touch you; with a full gauge late in the game, almost none can. With Spirit at zero, every curse takes. A SoulWard halves the harm to Spirit, and a Beehive, a Fresh Salve or a Soul Shard refills it.

Effigies

  • Carrying an effigy of the right color wards off its curses. When a curse comes for you, the effigy throws it back and is used up.
    • Their descriptions name the kinds: red for curses on movement, blue on using items, yellow for deadly curses, green for curses on searching. The table above gives each curse's color.
  • Using an effigy while cursed lifts the curses of its color.

Lifting a curse

Curses don't wear off. A curse stays on you, through saves and loads, until something lifts it. Some keep working at you meanwhile: Skeleton drains Vitality every 5 seconds, Blood hurts every 8 seconds while you touch blood, and Glutton makes you eat a food every 10 seconds.

Other help:

  • A Twisted Match senses curses in things.
  • The Cracked Bell destroys a curse set in an object, before it can reach you.
  • The Blood curse can't take hold in the graveyards at all.

At the judgment, being cursed counts against you. Lift every curse before the end.

3Possession

"Possession is to lose your usual self." One of your three desires can take you over. The Mirror's Possession tab says which, or "Not possessed for now…".

Greedwhen you see a thing, you can't resist taking it, and your Greed rises faster than usual
Attachmentwhen you see Information or Tales, you can't resist taking them, and your Attachment rises faster than usual
Destructiononce you start to attack, the urge to smash makes you strike three times, and your Destruction rises faster than usual

Possession doesn't wear off either, and only one desire can possess you at a time.

The cure is scent. "To lift it, you must eat something pungent." Eat one of the foods below; whether it works is a roll, and a stronger possession is harder to drive out. 4The Oni

The Oni is a demon that bargains: a pink, spectral spirit with wiry tentacles that floats about as it attacks. "HUMAN… THIS IS NO PLACE FOR YOUR KIND…" Beat it down and it stops to make a demand. To be spared, meet it:

  • Do something: hit it, burn it.
  • Don't do something: don't eat, don't use blood, don't use fire, don't fire a crossbow.
  • Give it something: food, blood, effigies or anything at all, "all of one type", meaning your whole stock of one kind of item. Or one item it has seen you carry.

Meet the demand and it lets you go: "I'll spare you now; next time, too, keep that attitude!!" Doing what it asks raises your desires: Greed when you feed it, Destruction when you hit it. Fall short and it wants more. An angry Oni wants half as much again.

Oni are met mostly in the graveyards. One that asks for every Reaper's Tooth you carry will spare you for them, but think twice: a Tooth is worth more to you than to it.

The sentence. At the third mark, the Reaper offers you a small chance.

  • Turn it down and you die on the spot. A Reaper's Tooth won't help.
  • Take it and the countdown starts, at that Reaper's time. You're sent to a graveyard, and a voice gives the rule: somewhere among the graves is a plate carved with your own name. Find it in time.     • The clock only runs while you're playing, not during scenes or in the menus.
    • The graves carry plates. Break them open to look.
    • Found it: the countdown stops, you earn 8,000 Fate, and the doors to the Pit open. Go through the Pit's door from the graveyard where you found your plate. Going into another graveyard first loses your plate's effect. Walk into the Pit, and you're back where the sentence began.
    • Time's up: the Reaper comes for you, and that's the end. No Tooth can save you.

6The graveyards

Under the land lie endless graveyards, "split by countless doors". No door leads there. You arrive by mishap:

  • A death sentence (section 5).
  • The hands. On some ground, when you crouch to pick something up or look at something, hands reach up out of the earth and grab you. They can't drag you in until about 15 minutes after you started or last came back from a graveyard, and they fail their first nine tries. After that each grab has a growing chance to drag you down. Being dragged in counts against you at the judgment. Once you know the nine names of the Graveyard (Fifteen Judgments), the hands can't take you.
  • A fall off the Cliff of the Fallen, until you know the nine names. After that, a fall only hurts.

What's different down there:

  • Which graveyard you land in is random, and they forget you: their graves, ghosts and items start fresh on every trip.
  • No save is offered while you move within a graveyard.
  • The Hell's Eye shows hidden things here, as the Third Eye does above.
  • The Grave Handbell calls someone who matters here.

Getting out

Every graveyard works the same way in outline:

  1. A notice tells you its rule when you arrive, and the talking graves add hints.
  2. Do what the rule asks. There are three kinds of rule, below. On a death sentence, the rule is your own plate (section 5).
  3. Get a door open. The doors stay sealed until the Gravekeeper opens one. A dark-red plate or the desire lumps go through it to Hell's messenger first.
  4. Reach the Pit: stairs down to a light in the floor. Step into it, and you're back above, where you left. After the question, the Keeper's door leads to a small graveyard whose two-eyed door opens onto the Pit.

Which rule you get is random, with two exceptions. Falls and drags from the late areas (Karma Grove, Land's End, the Gate of Judgment) always bring the desire lumps. And once you've delivered all nine dark-red plates, the plate rule doesn't come up again.

Rule 1: the grave's question

"Address the graves of those dwelling in them; one will question you. In reply, choose the true answer. It asks but once. If wrong, move on; try the next land."

Spoilerhow the question plays out

  4. A wrong answer ends it: that graveyard won't ask again. You'll have to try again in the next graveyard you reach.

Rule 2: the dark-red plate

"To leave this graveyard, you need a dark-red plate bearing a name. It is won by breaking a grave, but breaking it summons countless raging arms to bar you. If you win it, beyond the door give it to Hell's messenger." The name on it is one you'll know: one of nine, a different one each time.

Spoilerhow to get the plate out
  1. Find its grave with the Hell's Eye. One grave in each graveyard holds it, and only the eye tells it apart ("Someone's name seems written…", table below).
  2. Soften it with blood if it's hard: "Blood weakens the husk…"
  3. Break it. A mass of raging arms bursts out, cursing and grabbing at you.
  4. Drive the arms off with Shining Water. "Dark ones shun Shining Water…" Plain water does nothing. "When the arms of malice are gone, the dark-red plate shows."
  5. Take the plate on through the door. "Got the dark-red plate? Then on to the next door…" It takes you to Hell's messenger, in another graveyard: "Hand over that plate…" Once he has it, the way to the Pit opens.

Rule 3: three desire lumps

"Overhead, an egg-like pod. From it come three things: choose but one. Three places hold them. When all three are won, past the door, give to Hell's messenger."

Spoilerhow to collect the lumps
  1. Find the three pods hanging from the graveyard's trees: "Cocoons of the tree… inside a lump shaped like desire… in 3 spots… 3 in all…"
  2. Take one lump from each. Each offers three, one for each desire: the Apple Ingot, the Arrow Ingot and the Effigy Ingot. "Desire… Ruin, Attachment, Greed… Just grab what you fancy…"
  3. Carry all three through the next door to Hell's messenger. "Gather all 3 lumps… take the next door…" He reads your choice ("All show Greed…", "…all different…") and gives you something for it.

The Gravekeeper

"I am the Gravekeeper here… To open the next door, seek me… Strike the grave where I stand…"

Spoilerhow to find him and open the door
  1. Ring the great bell first (pull its rope): "Its toll startles the keeper…"
  2. Then find him. He has gone to ground in one of the big, hardened graves. Strike them until he comes out: "The keeper's in one of them… Strike the grave he's in… he comes…"
  3. Do his task. He asks you to open every mottled bud: "unless all these mottled buds bloom, it won't open." These are the big wrapped flowers growing out of the ground, not the small pods in the trees. A plain blow won't open one. Use a charged blow: the spear sticks, so waggle it free, and the bud blooms and dies.
  4. Pick a door. With every bud open, he offers to unseal one of two doors: "The right-eye door? The left-eye door?" Either will do.
  5. The small graveyard beyond has a one-eyed door back, another way into the nine-headstone cave, and the two-eyed door. "A two-eyed door… his job's done… Freely… take the one-eyed door…"
  6. The two-eyed door leads to the Pit. Step into the light.

The Grave Handbell in your items can be rung only in a graveyard, and only while the Keeper is about; otherwise it's greyed out. "Ring it to call the keeper…"

Three things to know before you go:

  • Each Fate Door is final. When you step through, everything behind it is gone for this playthrough. Finish what you want in an area first. "Past one door, the Fate Door is set… Before you pass it, finish what you must…"
  • Which doors appear depends on what you've done.
    • The shortcut: leave the First Land area with no keepsake from its ghosts (a letter or photo from the Altar of Hanging, or a painting) and none of their stories taken up, and the Fate Door takes you straight to Land's End. You skip most of the game.
    • From the Beheading Plaza, all three doors are open to you. The Fountain Plaza leads on to Swirling Desire. The door to Land's End skips everything between.
    • From the Desire Scaffold, the door to Karma Grove appears only if you've settled something with one of the ghosts of Karma Hall, or came by way of the Fountain Plaza. Otherwise the only way on is Land's End, which skips the finale in Karma Grove.
  • Two Fate checks. Leaving the Altar of Hanging needs 40,000 Fate, and leaving the Beheading Plaza needs 140,000. Short of that, the door sends you back to First Land.

Every room has a save point of sorts: the game offers a save each time you go through a door into another room (How the Game Works, section 12).

2The beginning

Fate Begins (the boat)

You start on a boat. A guide asks you questions on the way. Answer as you like.

Cave to Hell

The boat's journey ends in a cave.

  • The boatman is here. Talk to him at each spot.
  • A Skull Key lies nearby, and a skull box sits inside a barrel: break the barrel to reach it. The box offers an Apple, a Reaper's Tooth or an Arrow, and you may take only one. The Tooth is the one to take if you don't know which you'll need; it can save your life, and it matters later.
  • A Fate Door leads on to First Land.

3The First Land area

Four rooms. To leave, you need 40,000 Fate and the Fate Door at the Altar of Hanging.

First Land

A wide open place with a bell, where the game properly begins.

  • Who's here: a woman by the bell, who asks for a necklace; a man hanging from a tree, who hides something ("First, find the Tale"); a corpse hanging upside down.
  • A skull box with five choices: an Arrow, a Dried Salve, two paintings (Giese's Blue Painting and Gary's Red Painting) and a Glass Orb. One only.
  • Dangers: ghosts that come back; after a certain point, an ambush ("Something near… Just my imagination…?").
  • Doors: to Grudge-Desire Bog and to Bared Dark Hall.

Grudge-Desire Bog

A swamp with two wells side by side and skulls on pikes that taunt you.

  • The wells: each has a crank and a bucket. "Have you touched the wells? Their malice? Coming near won't wake them. But search deep inside, and…"
  • A Broken Necklace lies here.
  • Doors: to First Land and to Bared Dark Hall.

Bared Dark Hall

A long dark passage.

  • Who's here: a woman named Stella, who wants a necklace.
  • What's here: a Pearl Necklace, three Rusty Stakes and the Blue Winder.
  • A pendulum with heads on it: "On the swaying pendulum, each of three heads hid one stake. In light, uncover them."
  • A wall with handprints splits the passage: "a wall blood makes brittle; beyond it lies a hidden room."
  • Doors: to First Land, to Grudge-Desire Bog, and an iron drop door to the Altar of Hanging.

Altar of Hanging

  • Who's here: a woman hanging by a rope. "Burn her down: her malice ignites. Cut her down: her malice awakens. Which shall it be?" Cut down, she offers Linda's Letter or Linda's Photo: one, not both.
  • A skull box: a White Effigy, a Match or a Red Fruit.
  • The Fate Door to the prisons opens after the scene here, and needs 40,000 Fate. Check the shortcut rule above before you take it.

4The prisons

Four rooms of cells and executions. Iron Masks haunt the dark; light drives them off. Iron Masks to wear lie in three of these rooms; they have rules of their own, which the Information pages explain. To leave, you need 140,000 Fate and one of the three Fate Doors of the Beheading Plaza.

Warped Torture Cell

A great hall around a whirlpool.

  • Who's here: an interrogator, iron-masked ghosts, and a ghost in the whirlpool who wants something.
  • Five iron boxes hang over the floor, each big enough to hold a body.
    • Sacks hanging from the ceiling lower them. The Lever Arm opens a lowered box.
    • "The levers: when all five lids open, the sealed door opens too." That door leads to the Dark Cellar.
  • A Desire Wheel lies here.
  • Doors: to the Dark Cellar (two, one sealed at first) and to Ooze Killing Yard.

Dark Cellar

Cells under the floor.

  • Who's here: three heads: Kyle, Mick and Jeff. They want the three Rusty Stakes.
  • Two hanged corpses. Leave them whole: breaking one counts against you.
  • A skull box: two Information pages, the Fate Wheel or Fire Arrows.
  • A hidden nook behind a wall of handprints holds a chest.
  • Doors: to the Warped Torture Cell (two) and to Ooze Killing Yard.

Ooze Killing Yard

Where the ooze is made, around a turning floor.

  • The turning floor: "Turn floor valve. Floor turns as flesh flows." A ghost may wait on it.
  • What's here: the Red Landscape and the Blue Portrait.
  • Dangers: a hunting Reaper. Driving it off pays 10,000 Fate.
  • Doors: to the Warped Torture Cell, the Dark Cellar and the Beheading Plaza.

Beheading Plaza

Eight guillotines in a row, and an executioner in an iron mask.

  • The guillotines: "Iron Mask executioner still kills. 8 heads are in a row. Strike him down to stop it."
  • Who's here: a man named Alan.
  • What's here: Alan's Letter and Alan's Photo.
  • A skull box: the SoulWard, an Information page or the LifeWard.
  • Three Fate Doors open after the executioner's scene: to the Fountain Plaza, to Swirling Desire and to Land's End. Each needs 140,000 Fate. The Fountain Plaza is reachable only from here.

5The Fountain Plaza

A plaza around a fountain, off the main road.

  • Who's here: a ghost at the fountain. Which ghost depends on what you carry when you first arrive, so think about it before you come.
  • What's here: two letters, To My Friend and To Father.
  • Dangers: an ambush.
  • One door on, to Swirling Desire, opens after the fountain's scene. There's no way back.

6The lands of desire

Four rooms. The way on is from the Desire Scaffold.

Swirling Desire

  • Who's here: two women who want a necklace; a hanged corpse (leave it whole).
  • Dangers: a hunting Reaper (20,000 Fate to drive it off); swirls on the floor that drain your Vitality and block the way; hands at a door that snatch your items; and, once you've picked up a certain Tale here, an ambush.
  • Doors: two to Karma Hall, and one to Cliff of the Fallen that opens after a scene here.

Karma Hall

A long gallery of mirrors and candles.

  • Who's here: Linda again; a ghost who wants a painting; a music box and a girl.
  • The mirrors: "Seen in the mirror: yourself, or what lurks inside? Either way, you must look to know yourself and your bonds." Light the candles and look into the center mirror.
  • The music box needs a winder: "One screw plays pleasure, one screw plays grudge. A hidden third tune: when pleasure and grudge blend, it appears."
  • What's here: a Costly Trinket, the Oozy Gun, the Oozy Tooth and the Oozy Beehive (they matter for lowering your desires late in the game), and a Fate Wheel.
  • Passages within the hall open as you go. A wall of handprints hides a short passage to a door into Swirling Desire.
  • Dangers: an ambush.
  • Doors: to Swirling Desire and to Cliff of the Fallen (two each).

Cliff of the Fallen

A high cliff edge with five bodies.

  • The five bodies can be struck without it counting against you.
  • The cliff: a fall takes you to a graveyard, until you know the nine names of the Graveyard. After that, a fall only hurts.
  • What's here: a Desire Wheel.
  • Dangers: a hunting Reaper (30,000 Fate).
  • Doors: to Swirling Desire, to Karma Hall (two) and to the Desire Scaffold.

Desire Scaffold

  • Who's here: a ghost named Olnis, steeped in desire. He makes offers; you may take them or not.
  • Eye ornaments on the door can be broken while they're fragile.
  • Two Fate Doors open once Olnis's scenes are over: to Karma Grove and to Land's End. The door to Karma Grove needs more (see section 1). Taking the door to Land's End skips Karma Grove.
  • Dangers: an ambush.

7The end of the road

Karma Grove

  • Who's here: the girl from Karma Hall. Here she reveals a big secret, and asks something of you. Think before you answer: one answer ends the game on the spot.
  • An altar with a dish that takes a Reaper's Tooth. Read the glowing notice.
  • A skull box: three Tales; one only.
  • What's here afterward: a Dry Flower. It raises every desire when picked up, and carrying it into the Gate counts against you. See Fifteen Judgments before taking it.
  • Dangers: the last hunting Reaper (40,000 Fate).
  • Two Fate Doors, both to Land's End.

Land's End

The last open land, with a bridge, altars and a mirror.

  • Who's here: Olnis again.
  • The altars: seven dishes for protective items (wheels, eyes, wards) and three for the oozy items. Each placement lowers a desire.
  • The mirror: two misshapen candles can appear beside it. Fifteen Judgments explains when, and what the mirror can do, once.
  • A skull box: a Dagger, a Red Dagger or a Blaze Dagger.
  • What's here: a Fate Wheel and a Desire Wheel.
  • The bridge: jumping from its middle is said to lead to the graveyards (Evil7 §32).
  • The Fate Door to the Gate of Judgment.

The Gate of Judgment

The last room before the judgment. The save list calls it "The Gate".

  • The gatekeeper turns everything you carry into desire as you arrive. Anything you pick up in this room counts ten times over. Carry little and take nothing here.
  • The last Reaper fights you. Dodge its great scythe: being hit counts against you.
  • Then the judgment. The first time, the judge offers you one chance to start over.

8The graveyards

Under the land lie eight graveyards, each with two parts:

  • Hell's Graves: a field of graves without end, split by doors, with a Keeper who opens them.
  • the Graveyard: a smaller place around a ring of nine headstones.

Leaving one goes the same way each time: the graveyard's task done, the Gravekeeper unseals a door with one eye, which leads to a small graveyard. Its two-eyed door leads to the Pit, stairs down to a light; step into it, and you're back above, where you left. Dangers, section 6, has the details.

From any graveyard, the way back up is the Pit. There's no door into the graveyards from above; you get there by a mishap (Dangers, section 6), and which graveyard you land in is random. Their doors may lead to another graveyard, also at random.

Reading the tables

  • Fate is paid when you pick the item up.
  • G / A / D are the Greed, Attachment and Destruction it adds when you pick it up.
    • Use is what using it adds.
    • At the Gate is what each one you still carry adds when you enter the Gate of Judgment. Carry little into the Gate.
  • Boxes multiply desire. An item taken from a skull box or a jewelbox adds its desire once for each item the box offered. Fate is paid only once.
  • Where lists the places it lies. "Graveyards" means it turns up there on any trip. Many common items are in nearly every room, and some only appear after something happens.

How hard you hit

  • The tip and the charge decide the damage, not the shaft. A full charge does three to five times a plain thrust's damage, and only while you have some Spirit. Tips differ in what they do to different ghosts.
  • A charged blow to the head can drop a graveyard ghoul in one hit.
  • Playing as a woman, your blows do about three-quarters of a man's.
  • The Slaughter curse makes your hits half again as strong, but each one costs you the same Vitality (Dangers, section 2).
  • After clearing the game with rank S, charged blows do double damage.

Hungry Ghosts · PlayStation 2 · a player's guide to the endings

Fifteen Judgments最後の審判

At the end of Hungry Ghosts you are judged, and the judgment gives you one of fifteen ranks, S through N, each with its own ending. This guide explains what the judgment looks at, how to check where you stand during play, and how to steer a playthrough toward any rank.

Spoiler-light: characters are named and choices are described, but not what they mean. Places and items carry the names used by Mord of Swoonlight's English fan translation; the Japanese are in the table at the end. The rules were read out of the game's own code by Mord of Swoonlight's fan-translation project and checked against save files in play. A few route steps lean on Evil7's GameFAQs walkthrough of the Japanese release, marked (Evil7 §n).

1What the judgment counts

The judge looks at five things.

  1. Fate. A number that only ever goes up. The rank bands are at 2,200,000, 1,500,000 and 300,000.
  2. The three desires: Greed, Attachment and Destruction. Each is a running tally, judged in three bands. If any desire is very high, the high ones stop mattering.
Lowunder 20,000
High20,000 – 49,999
Very high50,000 or more
  1. A clean state. At the judgment you must not be cursed, and no desire may have possessed you.
  2. Deep karma. Two deeds near the end of the game: a choice in the finale at Karma Grove (route step 6), and learning all nine names in the Graveyard (step 8).
  3. The Seven Lures from Hell. Seven things the game counts against you. Each one, once done, stays on your record:
    • picking up a Purple Effigy;
    • destroying a hanged corpse (two in Dark Cellar, one in Swirling Desire; the five bodies at Cliff of the Fallen don't count);
    • giving Stella a necklace;
    • carrying the Dry Flower into the Gate of Judgment;
    • being dragged into a graveyard by the hands that grab at you;
    • being hit by the last Reaper's great scythe;
    • a ghost on the rotating floor of Ooze Killing Yard. It may never appear in a normal game.

3Checking where you stand

The menu screen (Select) has a Mirror page with three tabs: possession, desire and curses.

  • Desires. The desire tab always shows each tally as a number. Under 20,000 is low, 50,000 and up is very high.
    • Holding the Desire Wheel adds a page of text for each desire. Its wording steps up every 10,000. "Strong as 『Avarice』" means very high.
    • Desire Wheels lie in Warped Torture Cell, Cliff of the Fallen and Land's End.
  • Fate shows on that screen only while you hold the Fate Wheel. Without it you first see your Fate at the judgment.
    • Fate Wheels are in Dark Cellar (in a skull box, as one choice of four), Karma Hall and Land's End.
  • Curses and possession need no item. "Not cursed for now…" and "Not possessed for now…" are the clean state S needs.
  • Every save adds 200 Attachment. Save when you need to, not by habit.

4Fate, desires, and the tools that move them

Fate

Nothing lowers Fate. It comes from:

  • ghosts' scenes and the choices in them (the biggest single sums, up to 250,000);
  • picking up key items, documents and Info pages;
  • entering each room and area the first time, and opening each door the first time;
  • driving off the four hunting Reapers (10,000 to 40,000 each);
  • defeating ghosts.

Ghosts come back. Some come from spawners that refill a while after you clear them. Most refill after about 15 seconds, some after several minutes, so you can farm Fate. Every kill adds Destruction too, though.

Two Fate gates. Leaving the first area needs 40,000 Fate, and leaving the prisons needs 140,000. Without enough, the door sends you back to First Land.

Raising desires

  • Greed: food, salves, beehives and mushrooms you pick up.
  • Attachment: documents you read and take, protective items (eyes, wards, wheels, effigies), and saving.
  • Destruction: fighting, smashing things, picking up weapons. The Grave Gun alone adds 10,000.
  • Skull boxes and jewelboxes count an item's desire again for each other item the box offered. A three-item box counts it three times.
  • At the Gate of Judgment everything you still carry is turned into desire, from about 1,000 per key item up to 2,500 per protective item. Anything you pick up in that room counts ten times.

The mirror in Land's End is the latest reset, right before the Gate.

  1. Take the Dry Flower after the finale in Karma Grove. It adds 10,000 to every desire, so take it only if you will use the mirror.
  2. In Land's End, light the two misshapen candles that appear, and put the flower in the dish. That uses it up, so it never counts as a lure.
  3. Look into the mirror while holding at least one oozy item: the Gun (Destruction), the Tooth (Attachment) or the Beehive (Greed). They lie in Karma Hall and Land's End.
  4. Pick the one for your highest desire, then refuse to hand it over. That desire drops by 75%.

It plays only once. Looking in without an oozy item wastes it.

Accepting the imps' gift or Olnis's offers does the opposite: the imps double all three desires, and Olnis raises one by 20%.

The judge's one-time offer

The first time you stand before the judge, you are offered one chance to start over.

  • Accept, and you begin again at First Land with your Fate kept. Your desires, lures and items are wiped, and the rooms, documents and ghosts pay their Fate again.
  • Decline, and you are judged. Either way the offer never comes again.

This makes S much easier. Play a first pass any way you like, take the restart, then play the second pass clean. The Fate of both passes adds up.

5The S route in play order

This is a one-pass S: everything at once. It is demanding. If you have the patience for two passes, the judge's offer is the easier road.

  1. The cave after the boat

    • Talk to the boatman, then take the Reaper's Tooth from the skull box. You will want it in Karma Grove.

  2. The first area

    • First Land: its skull box offers five things; any will do.
    • Grudge-Desire Bog: crank a well and take the ring or the brooch. Keep the Soiled Photo from the man at the well. Carry the ring to the upside-down corpse in First Land. Take the Broken Necklace.
    • Back in First Land, give Sara the Broken Necklace.
    • Bared Dark Hall: meet Stella before you pick up the Pearl Necklace, and give her nothing. Then take the Pearl Necklace, the three Rusty Stakes and the Blue Winder.
    • Altar of Hanging: cut Linda down and take Linda's Letter (not the photo). Leave the area with at least 40,000 Fate.

  3. The prisons

    • Give the woman under the grate floor the Pearl Necklace. Her other scene plays only while you wear the Iron Mask; take its first option.
    • Never break the two hanged corpses in Dark Cellar.
    • Keep the stakes. Don't hand all three to Kyle, Mick and Jeff, and get their verdict for keeping them.
    • In Ooze Killing Yard, take the Red Landscape and/or the Blue Portrait.
    • Then walk from Ooze Killing Yard into Beheading Plaza. That walk, after the two steps above, is the only moment the game lets the music box take its good path later.
    • In Beheading Plaza, save all eight prisoners by shooting the executioner at each guillotine (Evil7 §25). Give Alan Linda's Letter, and take both Alan's Letter and Alan's Photo.
    • Leave with at least 140,000 Fate through the Fate Door to Fountain Plaza. The plaza's Fate Doors go one way.

  4. Fountain Plaza

    • You arrive on a small bridge facing a sturdy door with three rusted holes; use the three Rusty Stakes on it.
    • The ghost at the fountain is chosen by what you carry when you first arrive. With Alan's things in hand it is Linda.
    • Hand her Alan's Letter and take the first option every time.
    • Take the letters To My Friend and To Father.

  5. The desire area

    • Karma Hall:
      • Talk to Linda while Alan's Photo is the only one of the four letters and photos you still hold.
      • In the mirror you are asked for To My Friend. Refuse: this is the biggest single Fate award in the game, 250,000.
      • Play the music box with the Blue Winder.
      • Give the painting ghost the painting it asks for.
      • Take the Costly Trinket and at least one oozy item.
    • Swirling Desire: with no necklace left in hand, take Stella's first option. Leave the hanged corpse whole.
    • Desire Scaffold: break Olnis's eye ornaments and decline each offer. Give him the Costly Trinket when you meet him. Then take the Fate Door to Karma Grove.

  6. Karma Grove

    • Drive off the last hunting Reaper.
    • At the finale a girl reveals a big secret and asks you to go with her. Refuse. Going with her ends the game on the spot. Then win the fight that follows.
    • Take the Dry Flower only if you will use the mirror in Land's End.
    • Place the Reaper's Tooth on the altar as late as you can.

  7. Land's End

    • Olnis tells you nine names. Remember them; the Graveyard needs them.
    • The mirror reset, on your highest desire.
    • The dishes for Attachment, giving up the Fate Wheel last (check your Fate first).

  8. One last graveyard trip

    • You can jump off the bridge in Land's End to get there (Evil7 §32).
    • In the Graveyard, take the nine name plates.
    • In Hell's Graves, hand the imps 33 Imp Bones and refuse their gift on your highest desire.

  9. The Gate of Judgment

    • Carry as little as you can, and pick nothing up in the Gate's room.
    • Dodge the last Reaper's great scythe.
    • Check the Mirror: every desire under 20,000, not cursed, not possessed.
    • Decline the judge's offer to start over.

Throughout: never pick up a Purple Effigy or the Grave Gun. Keep away from the graveyard hands until you know the nine names; after that they can't drag you. Save sparingly, and heal after each reset that drains your Spirit and Vitality.

Fate along the way (rough):

200,000after the first area
600,000at Beheading Plaza
750,000after the fountain
1,650,000after the desire area
2,000,000at Land's End
2,450,000after the graveyard trip

Without that trip a one-pass run falls short of 2,200,000.

6Points of no return

Each of these is fixed for the rest of the playthrough (until the judge's restart).

  1. Every skull box gives one item. The rest vanish with the box.
  2. Linda's Letter or her photo, not both.
  3. Leaving the first area with none of these: Linda's Letter or photo, Gary's or Giese's painting from First Land's skull box, the Broken Necklace given to Sara, or Stella met with a necklace in hand. Without one, the exit sends you straight to Land's End, past most of the game.
  4. The first iron-masked ghost you deal with decides what the masked interrogator says.
  5. Handing all three stakes to Kyle, Mick and Jeff. You lose their other verdict and the key to Fountain Plaza.
  6. The walk from Ooze Killing Yard into Beheading Plaza (route step 3). Leave the prisons without it and the music box can't take its good path, so no S.
  7. Beheading Plaza's Fate Doors. Only the door to Fountain Plaza goes past the fountain. The door to Swirling Desire skips it, and the door to Land's End skips the desire area and Karma Grove too.
  8. The fountain's ghost (Linda, Gary or Giese) is fixed on your first arrival by what you carry: the Red Landscape alone brings Gary, the Blue Portrait alone brings Giese, anything else brings Linda.
  9. Handing over To My Friend or To Father when asked rules out S.
  10. Linda in Karma Hall plays one scene, chosen by which of her letters and photos you hold.
  11. Desire Scaffold's Fate Door to Karma Grove appears only after a run through the fountain. The other door skips the finale.
  12. Going with the girl at the finale ends the game.
  13. The mirror in Land's End plays once.
  14. The judge's offer comes once, and declining it uses it up.

The Seven Lures are permanent too.

7The other ranks

A  Fate 2,200,000 or more, nothing else matters. Play the S route with its graveyard trip, and don't worry about desires or lures. Accepting the imps' gift pays 50,000 more Fate than refusing it.

B C D  Fate 1.5M–2.2M. Play the S route without the graveyard trip, and hand over To My Friend when asked. That ends near 1,800,000. If your Fate creeps toward 2,200,000, skip optional pickups. If a death sentence sends you to a graveyard, find your own plate and leave.

  • B: no desire at 50,000 or more, and no lure. Use Olnis, the altar, the mirror and the dishes to keep them down.
  • C: let one desire pass 50,000. Attachment is the easiest: pickups and reading add up to about 130,000 before any reset.
  • D: as B, plus one lure. Picking up a Purple Effigy is the simplest.

E–L  Fate 300,000–1.5M. Play the first area and the prisons as the S route does, then leave Beheading Plaza by the Fate Door to Land's End. That ends somewhere around 600,000–900,000. The letter then depends on your desires:

8Names in this guide

The English names are the ones the translation shows in the game. Each one had to fit in the space the original Japanese text takes up in the game's data, because the patch can't make room that isn't there. A Japanese character takes two bytes and an English letter one, so a name gets about twice as many letters as the Japanese had characters, and fewer in the tightest places. That is why some names are shorter or plainer than a free translation would make them: 終わりの大地 is "Land's End" rather than "the Land Where It Ends", and 地獄への墓場 is "Hell's Graves" rather than "the Graveyard on the Road to Hell". A few item names got a little more room by reusing space the game never shows.
